    Is the output of the generative AI system protected by intellectual property rights?
    https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-gb/knowledge/publications/f237e6c7/is-the-output-of-the-generative-ai-system-protected-by-intellectual-property-rights

    United States (USCO Rules)
    https://pec.ac.uk/blog_entries/copyright-protection-in-ai-generated-works/

        No Human Authorship = No Copyright:
        The U.S. Copyright Office requires a human author for a work to qualify
        for copyright protection. Software generated entirely or predominantly
        by AI without substantial human creativity is placed into the public
        domain (meaning anyone can use, copy, or modify it).

        Human-Assisted Code:
        If a human programmer writes architectural designs, edits, refactors,
        heavily structures, or creatively combines AI-generated snippets, the
        human-authored portions can be copyrighted, while the raw AI-generated
        blocks remain unprotected.

        Prompts Alone Are Insufficient: Simply writing a natural-language prompt
        or "vibe coding" an entire application does not grant copyright over the
        resulting code.


    United Kingdom (CDPA 1988 Rules)
    https://www.farrer.co.uk/news-and-insights/protecting-code-and-creativity-software-ip-rights-and-ai-generated-code/

        Computer-Generated Works (Section 9(3)): Unlike the US, the UK Copyright,
        Designs and Patents Act 1988 explicitly recognizes computer-generated works
        (CGWs) that have no traditional human author.

        Who Owns It: Authorship and ownership go to "the person by whom the arrangements
        necessary for the creation of the work are undertaken" (often the person or company
        that ran/directed the AI system).

        gov.uk Copyright and AI: Consultation
        https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence
